Laos III: The Thakhek Loop

Published ___day, June _, 2025

<<< >>>

In this leg of our trip, we travel with our slow boat friends from the capital of Laos, Vientiane, to a city further south called Thekhek. Here we will start the Thakhek Loop, a loop that goes through some pretty nature with some homestays along the way. I'm excited for this because the area is directly west of Phong Nha, Vietnam, an area with nature Kaila and I really enjoyed. It also feels a buit like redemption to us because we skipped the famous Ha Giang motorbik loop in north Vietnam because of a combination of poor weather and our lack of motorbike experience at the time.

Monday, May 26 - Arriving in the capital

Kaila, Sam, Josh, Jarryd, and myself arrive to Vientiane from Vang Vieng, Laos via high speed train around 3pm. We hire a minivan to take us to the city center. Sam, Josh, and Jarryd are staying in a hostel called Sailomyen Cafe and Hostel, where dropped off. Kaila and I booked a private room at a hostel a couple minutes up the street, so we walk over there to check in and drop our bags, then go back to their hostel for lunch/dinner. Elsa meets us here too! She got in last night and she still has a few hours before her flight tonight back to Sweden. I get fried chicken fried rice that I'm convinced was actually fried fish, but was tasty regardless. Once we finish eating, we walk over towards the riverside market. Along the way, we pass a cafe that is in the main floor of an abandoned building. Sam goes in to check it out, and we wait outside, until he is in there a suspicious amount of time and Kaila and I go in to check on him. We find him ordering some bread (which feels very Montreal of him) then wander the building a bit more before continuing to the river. The others split off with Elsa to get her bag from her hostel. We get to a park on the riverside where there are lots of people hanging out. Some loud Lao music is playing, and there are kids running around and families walking by. We continue towards the market, then coordinate with the others to meet them at the food market a couple blocks up to say goodbye to Elsa before she goes to the airport.

For almost the whole time the slow boat group has been traveling together, we have talked about having a night where we get a bunch of fruit to try together. There are some different fruit stands around the food market, so this feels like a perfect opportunity! We get some mangosteens, lychees, rambutans, and durian, then take a lap through the aisles of all the different food vendors before deciding it hasn't been long enough since our late lunch for us to be hungry for much more food for now (though Sam and I make a note to come back for some scallion pancakes). We find a seating area in a back courtyard of the market and sit down in some low chairs and eat our fruit. I've had lychees and mangosteen before, but Josh teaches us how to open each with your hands. When I got the mangosteen in Hoi An, I had to use a pocket knife to get them open. For the lychees, he shows us how to minimize getting the juices to get on your hands. The rambutans are new to me! They are round, egg sized fruit with a purple skin covered in green spikes/hairs, almost resembling dragonfruit. Also like dragonfruit, they are a bit underwhelming compared with their dramatic appearance. They taste like lychees, but a little less flavorful. The skin is thicker, so we can't use the trick Josh taught us to open them. I've never had durian before, but I don't find the smell overwhelming. I don't think they're even that stinky. Josh tells us that when he asked if they had spoons, the vendor said "No, only sock" then handed him a plastic glove. The taste is complex, and I understand how it would be an acquired taste for some, but I like it. There are lots of different flavors that seem to come in flashes as I'm eating it. Sam said that each bite tasted different, which I aslo think is true. I think generally I would describe it as somewhere between a banana and a mango but buttery. The texture is also between banana and mango, but it's also creamy. Hard to describe! I think I'll have to eat some more before I try to describe it better. While we're eating our fruit, some friendly cats come by to visit us. There's a momma cat and some kittens, and most of them have collars on. Sam and I eventually go back into the aisles to get a couple scallion pancakes, splitting one with egg and one with chicken.

Kaila and I split off after about an hour and a half to go to an Italian restaurant called Acqua that has GF carbonara, then walk back over to the night market since we didn't see it much before we reunited with the others. I'm looking for a polyster blend shirt that might not hold as much sweat as the cotton shirts I brought and am tired of. We laugh about we overpacked but still somehow don't like the clothes we brought. Eventually I find a cheap shirt that feels like polyster and has the word "sports" embroided on the front in the place a logo or pocket might usually be. I think that's funny, so I get it and hope for the best. We walk back to our hostel after this, and as we're walking by the Sailomyen hostel the others are staying at, who do we see next door except our German pal Jan! He tells us he's staying at the same hostel, and gives us an update on the other German guy Jacum who they brought down here from Vang Vieng yesterday after he was convulsing and exhibiting some scary symptoms after tipsy tubing. I think everyone had it in their heads that it could be methanol poisoning, but apprently the doctors ruled that out. Jan says he's leaving tomorrow for south Thailand, so I guess this is the last time we will run into him. We say goodbye to him and go back to our hostel to sleep.

Tuesday, May 27 - To Thakhek!

Today we take the bus to Thakhek! We organized it through Sam, Josh, and Jarryd's hostel, so we all plan to meet there at noon. Kaila and I get up early so we can get breakfast. I have a super mid omelete and some instant coffee provided at the hostel, then we get some coffee at the abandoned building cafe on our way to a place called CoCo & Co where Kaila gets a smoothie bowl and some potato wedges. Fuel! We go to a grocery store next to get some snacks for the Loop, the strangest of which I got are "shredded chicken and seaweed bread". This ends up being not very good because it has a strange sweet custard with it. There was a spicy version that I regret not getting because I think it might have done a better job at balancing the sweetness.

Once we get our snacks, we quickly go back to our hostel to pick up our bags and drop off our keys, then arrive at the other hostel at 12:10pm, just as the minivan driver arrives to take us to the bus station. The bus leaves right at 1pm. It's six hours to Thakhek, which passes pretty slowly but gives me some good time to work on the blog. We arrive to the Thakhek bus station around 7pm, then get a tuk tuk to the town center. The driver fits seven of us into the kind of tuk tuk that has the inward facing bench seats in the back and a big beefy motorcycle front, but we also each have big backpacks which take a good chunk of the back of the seating and still hang out a concerning amount, even with the cord the driver ties on top. There's not room for all seven of us in the back, so Sam and I take seats up front next to the driver and facing outward, which feels pretty crazy.

We do make it to the hostel though! We open up the gate to the Bike and Bed and are immediately greeted by two stumpydogs who we learn are Ashy and Lucky. Ashy is immediately riled up and jumping and trying to play which is cute. We were able to book five beds in a six person dorm, which means it will be Kaila, Sam, Josh, Jarryd, me, and possibly a rando. We are surpised to see a seventh bed in the room, which we are told is for one of the folks that runs the hostel, which is pretty funny.

We drop off our bags and head to get dinner at a spot called Laotown and get a bunch of dishes to share including some chinese broccoli, fermented pork ribs, spicy noodle salad, chicken wings, coconut shrimp, and spicy pork. The food is good and cheap! After this, we walk to get some ice cream, then we head back to the hostel to go to bed because we plan to start the loop tomorrow.

Tuesday, June 3

Today we got up early for our 9:30am flight to Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, where we'll spend a few days exploring before heading to Malaysian Borneo. We leave before the hotel-provided breakfast, but they say they'll pack us some fruit and bread. This is nice, but they unfortunately packed the bread on top of the fruit in the same containers which meant Kaila couldn't eat it. We get to the airport about two hours before boarding, and the ticket counter for our flight check in isn't open yet, but we only have to wait 20ish minutes. The airport is not very busy, so it's a quick process then easy sailing through security into the small airport that only has about ten gates. We get some coffee and Kaila gets some yogurt, then eventually board our plane for the three hour flight to KL. More on that soon!
