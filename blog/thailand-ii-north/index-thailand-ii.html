In this leg of our trip, we take a bus from Siem Reap, Cambodia into Bangkok, where we stay for a few days before taking a plane to the northern city Chiang Mai, where we take a much needed break before another minivan trip to Chiang Rai another city in the north close to the Laos border.

Friday, May 2nd - Hellish customs then Bangkok

The time on the road from Siem Reap is pretty quick and easy, but the border crossing takes a lot more time and energy. It takes us about three hours to reach the border, and then we are through Cambodia customs pretty quickly, but waiting in line for the customs into Thailand takes us fully another two hours in a hot and not air-conditioned room. Once we're back on the road, it's three and a half more hours on the road and we arrive in Bangkok!

We're staying at a hostel in a neighborhood sort of on the edge of the city, which we don't realize is as much of a commute into the city center until we arrive at the train station and have to figure out how to get to our hostel. Turns out we have to take two separate trains to get out to it! This is also how we learn that Bangkok has more than one train system. We are near a Metro station, so that's the first system we experience. It's nice! We have a few different options for entering the station, and Google Maps even tells us which entrance is best based on the direction we're going. We're able to get through the fare gates by simply tapping our Visa credit card. The platforms have glass doors that separate people on the platform from the train tracks, and only open once the train arrives. We ride for about a half hour, then get to a station where we'll transfer. This is where we learn about SkyTrain, another one of Bangkok's train systems, but we have to exit this station before we can transfer. We tap our Visa cards on the fair gates to exit, then go out to the street, and take a staircase up to the elevated tracks (we realize later that we could have gone up some escalators from the subway). Unfortunately, we can't tap our Visa cards here. The ticket machines and counters are cash only, and we don't have any of the local currency (Thai Baht). We go back down to the street to find an ATM, and luckily there is a mall connected to the stations that has a bank. We get some cash (after having to try some different machines), then go back up to the elevated station and immediately feel dumb because there is an ATM at the top of the escalators we bypassed by coming up from the street instead of through the mall that connects the Metro and the SkyTrain. This ends up being like a 40 minute detour, but we eventually get some tickets (which are these cool little cards) then ride out to the SkyTrain station our hostel is on. I wonder if we will be able to reload our single ride cards, but the machine takes the cards as we exit, so I guess they've already worked that out!

We're staying at a place called Holy Sheet Hostel. The area it's in seems pretty quiet. The SkyTrain is above the main street in the neighborhood here, but still only some restaurants and businesses with more car traffic than foot traffic. There are some side streets/alleys that branch off here, and our hostel is on one of these. We walk in to a bar area with a few people sitting at a couple different tables. One of these people get up and asks if we're checking in, then we follow her upstairs to the reception desk. This floor is long, with a living room area on the street side with some folks watching a movie, then in a line from there is a big picnic style table with benches, the reception desk, then a kitchen area. The women that checks us in grabs some keys and tells us to follow her. We expect her to go up some stairs across from the reception desk, but she actually just opens a door at the top of the stairs we just came up, right next to the kitchen, and our room is right here! Kind of a surprise, but convenient I guess! We're pretty tired at this point, so once we're in the room, we get ready and go to bed.

Saturday, May 3rd - Some temples and a weekend market

In the morning, we get up and I have some of the provided breakfast, which consists of a couple hard-boiled eggs and some toast with orange jam. We take the SkyTrain to the Metro to get back to the city center. There are a couple different temples we want to check out. The first is Wat Arun, from the 17th century, and we are booking it over there because it's a common destination for Thai tourists to visit and take photos in traditional costumes. It's a pretty temple with a large, tiled stupa in the center. There are some statues around the base of the stupa in a way that looks like they're holding it up, and even they are tiled!

Wat Arun is right across the river from the Grand Palace, so we take a quick ferry across the river that only costs the equivalent of a fraction of a dollar. When we get over to the Palace though, we see that it's closed for Coronation Day. Near a sign that explains the closure, a Thai man starts chatting with us and hands us a map, showing us some other temples we can go to that would be open and free today because it's Buddha Day. One of the temples he mentions is the "Black Buddha". I am ready to hop in a tuk tuk he beckons over, but Kaila is suspicious and starts googling, then learns about the common "Black Buddha Scam" in Thailand, where someone gets you to a tuk tuk to a small temple that is always free (not just on Buddha Day). After that, they take you to a suit shop and strongly encourage you to buy something before letting you get back on the tuk tuk. We actually met someone later in our trip that went through with this up to the suit shop, but he just left the shop instead of buying something and getting back on the tuk tuk. Anyway, we didn't get on the tuk tuk, and instead go over to a temple next to the Palace called Wat Pho, which is known for a huge sleeping Buddha statue! The building with the Buddha is right by the entrance, and it really is big. Probably around 30 meters long. It's cool to be around a statue of this scale that is oriented along the ground instead of vertically, like most statues this size would be. Being able to walk around it helps to get a grip of the scale. Once we get around to the feet, we can see some intricate patterns inlaid into the soles of the feet with shimmering mother of pearl.

After we feel like we have spent a sufficient amount of time gawking at this huge statue, we explore the rest of the complex for an hour or so. There are some some pretty gardens, old looking bonsais, statues, and more tiled stupas throughout the complex. It feels like everywhere you look, there is some crazy detailed piece of art.

After we finish at Wat Pho, we walk over to a restaurant that shows gluten free pad thai on Kaila's GF app. It's called Thipsamai, and it turns out to be the restaurant that invented pad thai! Quite the surprise. They have a whole operation. There's a line out the door, but it's moving fast. Once we get towards the front, they hand us a menu, and we order before we even sit down. Their original pad thai here is the tamarind-sauced noodles with small dried shrimp and some big prawns. Instead of having scrambled eggs mixed in though, all the noodles are wrapped in an extremely well crafted omelet that's so thin on the top you can see the noodles through it. It's very impressive, and delicious.

After lunch, we take the subway to a weekend market called Chattawatch Market. This market is different than most of the other markets we've been to so far, in part because it's absolutely huge, but also because there is a mix of stalls and shops. There is one big road in a horsheshoe that is lined with vendors, but there are also aisles/paths/hallways that branch of from here and wind through shops, restaurants, and cafes, completely covered, which is nice because it looks like it might rain. We walk a bit on the road and see some kids playing music, then spend over two hours zigzagging through the market, looking through vintage clothes stalls, small clothing brands, shops with knick knacks, bags and backpacks, all kinds of stuff. Kaila gets a fresh coconut at a stall, and eventually we come to the art section, which is essentially a bunch of gallery spaces connected together. We find some really cool artists including a guy who makes really cool digital collages. We get a piece of art: a woodblock print of some lotus flowers from an artist who carves impressive designs even though his arms end at his elbows.

It sprinkles on and off while we walk through, but eventually it starts to rain a bit harder, which we take as our cue to head back to the subway. There's a big food market on our way back to the hostel, so we decide to stop on our way back. We take the escalator up from the subway station for the market and suprisingly exit into a huge mall, with floors above and below us. We walk around and eventually find an exit, and walk around the back of the giant building to find the food market in what appears to be an old parking lot. The food vendors are in tents forming a perimeter with some bigger tents in islands on the inside of the perimeter. We take a lap around the border, and I'm feeling overwhelmed by the choices, but one thing I keep seeing are these bowls that are packed tall with what looks like ribs covered in herbs. Some of these stacks are huge, obviously made to hare. We check out one of the big restaurant tents in the middle, and they give us a menu where I find the dish: leng zabb. We google it and learn it's a dish of pork bones stewed in a spicy chili broth. This restaurant has a few different sizes of bowls on the menu, so I order the smallest so I can try it, along with a thai tea in case the soup is too spicy. Once the food arrives, I realize the small bowl might still be intended for sharing. It's a lot of food, and it's a looooot of chilis. It comes with a spoon and some plastic gloves for holding the pork bones, which mostly seem to be back bones, but there is still a decent amount of meat on them. The soup is indeed spicy and I'm grateful for the thai tea. Eating it is a process, but I'm able to eat the meat off all the bones and I finish what feels like a brave amount of the soup.

We go back into the mall which gives me a chance to cool off from what is by far the spiciest meal I've had on the trip so far. We go down to the food court here to find some food for Kaila, and decide to get some food from a grocery store that's also down here, then head back to the subway and make our long commute back to the hostel.

Sunday, May 4th - A floating market (sort of)

Today is our last full day in Bangkok, so we decide we're going to go out to one of the floating markets outside the city. First though, some more boiled eggs and toast for me then we walk over to a dedicated GF bakery called Tama GF for Kaila's breakfast. She get's a gluten free pistachio croissant, a cheese roll, and a brioche cream pastry that she says is the best GF pastry she's ever had. Once we're fueled up, we start our journey out to the market that folks online said is better than some of the markets right outside of Bangkok, called Ampawa Market. However, it is a journey to get to without a tour or a car. First we get a Grab to the subway, then we take the subway out to a station nearest a bus station that the Ampawa Market website says has minivans out to the market. Even though this is the nearest subway station, we still have to get a taxi out to the bus station. Once we're there, we go inside of what seems a bit like an airport and a bit like a mall and go around a bunch of ticket booths, but no one knows about the Ampawa minivan. We realize we must be in the wrong place, so we go back outside and find an area where some smaller buses are parked near some other ticket booths. Eventually we find one that says Ampawa, but there's no one at the booth. We ask the person at the booth next door, and they said the vans all left already, which doesn't seem right because the market is supposed to peak between 6 and 9pm and it's only 2pm. We continue to ask around with the help of Google Translate, and eventually someone tells us the person that used to run the minivans out there doesn't run them anymore. This is unfortunate for us because we're still like 1.5 hours out from the market. We realize the best option at this point is to try to neogiate a taxi out there, so we go to the taxi bay and find a driver who agrees to take us out there, wait for us, then drive us back for 1500 baht (around 45 USD) which feels fair, so we get in his taxi.

Now maybe we have made some questionable choices before this point, but this is where I feel like we start to make an error. When we arrive at the market, our driver parks us somewhere that really doesn't feel like a market. He walks us to] the river with the man who's shop I believe we just parked at, and we can see what looks like market tents up the road from here. However, we don't see any market boats, which is what we expect from pictures of the market. We wonder if we just can't see the market boats from here, so we ask the shop guy about boats, and he walks us over to what is essentially a giant canoe with like ten benches, a roof, and a guy on the back with a motor attached to a long propeller (there's probably a better name for this kind of boat, but I'm not a boat guy). He tells us the price is 1,000 baht (30 USD) for us to be taken on the boat through the market and around the "island" he shows us on a map, which actually just looks like the area of land defined by the Mekong River and a canal that runs out of it then back in. He says it will be a two hour ride. At the time, this seems like the best option for us since we're already out here. Even though the price seems high, we are only two people, so it doesn't seem unbelievable, and we're able to get the price down to 800 baht. It's clear now that we should have just walked over to the market tents at this point, but instead we pay our taxi driver for half the trip, get on the boat, and start our journey that we think is out to the market boats.

We start on the Mekong River, and approach the area where we saw the market tents. This is right at one end of the canal we saw on the map, so we think maybe the market boats are around the corner. However, when we enter the canal, we see people up on paths on either side of the water, about three meters up the bank, and it seems like there are restaurants lining the paths behind. A few bridges arch up over the canal to connect the paths. We are feeling confused because it feels like a market, but there are no market boats. There are a few other boats like ours, but they are empty and on the side of the canal here at areas where there are steps going up to the paths. This is when we start to wonder if we are being scammed and if there is even a floating market at all.

The guy driving the boat starts yelling out to us as we're passing this area with the people on the paths above. He points ahead, and at first I think he's telling us to get out, but then I realize he's pointing at something in the water. It's a huge monitor lizard! It's probably 1.5 meters from head to tail. We keep going and even see some more swimming in the canal. I'm surprised they're all right here, because the canal can't be more than ten meters across and looks shallow, but maybe the lizards are able to get scraps from the restaurants here. At this point, I'm thinking that even if we got scammed, at least we got to see some giant lizards.

We continue down the canal, and every time we round a corner, I think "The floating market must be right up here", although I think I know that we have already seen the "floating" market. Eventually we get back to the Mekong River and pull up to the dock where we launched from. The boat docks, and we ask the driver if we pay him, but he says no and points to the area where we talked with the shop guy. However, he is not there. At this point, we are feeling scammed, so we decide to continue to walk to the market, assuming we will still have to run into the shop guy later when we go back to our taxi. Although that makes us wonder if our taxi will even still be there, or if this is somehow part of a larger scheme. Perhaps the Black Buddha near miss from the other day is making us paranoid.

We walk over to the market tents we see from the boat dock. There is a sort of central vein road with tents on either side that intersects the canal, with some side paths on either side of the canal that we could see from the boat. We walk up one of these, and hear some folks trying to sell boat rides for 500 baht. We try to ask a guy if there are any market boats, but we aren't able to adequeately ask the question even with Google Translate, and the guy just tells us about the 500 baht boats. We do some googling, and it seems like the market went through a bit of hard times during COVID, and never really recovered to its floating status. Sad, but even though there aren't any market boats, the market here is still pretty good. There are some clothes and trinkets, but it's mostly food, which Kaila and I both find to be the most interesting type of market. I'm able to get some good street food here: some fried meat that came with a tangy sauce and some fried greens, meat balls on a skewer that I thought were fish balls but were actually pork, and a bunch of fresh, mystery dumplings (they weren't marketed like that, I just can't understand Thai). Eventually, we even see a pair of market boats appear from somewhere, and they're making some kind of noodles on a wok in one of the boats. So even though the market wasn't floating, it was still a good experience.

Now the only thing that's left is to go back and see if our taxi is there, and if it is, face the shop guy that overcharged us for a boat ride to see the floating market that was almost completely on land. At this point, we wonder if we should just cut our losses and try to find a new taxi so we don't have to pay the shop guy for the boat, but this feels wrong since we already took the ride, and we're not even sure if we could get a taxi all the way to the Metro from here. When we're just up the street, we see that our taxi is still there, and our driver tells us we didn't pay for our boat. We find the guy, and tell him that everyone at the market was charging 500 baht. He argues that we already agreed to 800 baht, but at this point we tell him that we will only pay 500. A woman appears and starts arguing with him, and shows us a calculator with 650 on the display. We stay firm at 500 and eventually they take it from us and we get in the taxi. It is a bit awkward, but it seemed predatory that we were taken to this farther out dock instead of to the place just up the street where all the other boats of the same size were advertising the same price. Luckily, the taxi driver still takes us out to the closest Metro station 1.5 hours back towards Bangkok, and we take the subway back to the city center so Kaila can get some food.

Kaila picks a restaurant called So Samsen for dinner, which is near Khao San Road, a street notorious in Bangkok for partying. Kaila gets some noodles for dinner and we share a papaya salad, then go over to Khao San. We expect it to be like the party street in Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam, and it mostly is. I think the street in HCMC was a bit more impressively lit up and packed, but that may be because it was a bit denser. Khao San Road actually spans two separate roads in the area. We walk around for a bit, seeing the streams of tourists in front of bars playing loud music with chairs on the street, and sometime live music or dancers to try to convince people to sit down for drinks. There are some food carts too, as well as tents selling souvenirs or small tables stacked with jars of weed, packs of joints, and brightly colored edibles. At each end of the streets we walk down are many guys in tuk tuk holding up flyers for the infamous "ping pong shows" (googling this will almost certainly be NSFW). After we do a lap, we decide to dit down for a drink at a bar with some live music and listen while we have a drink. We feel like we've had enough transit time today, so we finish our drinks then get a Grab back to our hostel instead of the Metro-SkyTrain journey.

Monday, May 5th - Finishing Strong in Bangkok

Today we're flying to the northern Thai city Chiang Rai, but that's not until the evening, so we decide to spend the first chunk of the day exploring some more of the city. We pack our things, I have my last boiled egg and toast meal (I hope at least) and we check out and stash our bags at the reception desk. The Grand Palace is open again today, we we go there first and see the adjoining Temple of the Emerald Buddha. We take the Metro/SkyTrain into the city center and get to the Palace complex to buy tickets. While we're in line, I'm told I can't wear shorts, but the folks working showing me to a desk where I can buy some pants to wear. At first I only see the elephant patterned pants that I see every other tourist wearing, and I'm feeling dread that I may have to dissolve my pride and buy a pair at an exhorbinate price, but luckily they are only the equivalent of six USD and there is an all blue pair without elephants. We explore the temple and I don't recall ever seeing an Emerald Buddha, but we do see some huge guard statues. I like these guys that look kinda scared:

Around the edge of the central space of the temple, there is a wrap-around mural depicting some Hindu/Buddhist stories, sort of like the visual stories we saw at Angkor Wat and the Royal Palace in Cambodia. This was my favorite section of this mural, which had an encryption with it that discussed how the god put out their tongue to protect the city while they slept, but then was killed when an enemy struck their tongue with a discus.

It's really hot, so we don't last long in the Temple/Palace. We walk to a restaurant called Baan Tha Tien to get some lunch: shrimp pad thai and panang curry. Once we're refueled and have had the opportunity to cool off, we're feeling brave enough to go outside again and walk over to a market called Ang Ong, which feels like an alley turned into a market. It's very tight quarters, yet somehow there are still folks on motorbikes squeezing their way through!

The market leads us to an area called the Songwat, where we find a cute cafe called Songwat Coffee Roasters to get a coffee. I like this area a lot! There's a lot of street art and it fun to walk around looking for cool murals.

Walking through Songwat brings us to Bangkok's Chinatown, which is also aesthetically pleasing. By now, it's about time for us to go back to our hostel to get our stuff and go to the airport, so we walk to the nearest Metro station, take a subway to the SkyTrain for the last time.

After we pick up our bags, we get a Grab to the airport, getting there early enough that we have some time to check out a lounge Kaila has access to with the Capital One card. We get some free food there (I have some crispy fish and fried chicken), then we get on our plane. It's dark when we're leaving Bangkok, and Kaila get a cool picture of the city's lights which I think helps show how huge Bangkok is.

We land in Chiang Mai around 9:15pm, so it's already too dark to see our surroundings when we drive to our hostel. We're staying at a place called Ma's Guesthouse, which is in a part of town called the Old City, which is in a square shaped area about 1.5 kms to a side that used to be enclosed in a wall and a moat. The moat still exists (it's only about 10 meters across) and there are apparently certain areas where you can see the ruins of the wall. But we don't see any of that tonight! Ma has waited up for us, so as soon as she shows us to our room, it's off to bed for us.

Tuesday, May 6th - A much needed rest day

When we wake up up in Chiang Mai, we realize that neither one of us feels like we have the energy to do much of anything today. I think the combination of a pretty intense three days of exploring Bangkok in the heat plus the stretched out days of commuting into and out of the central part of the city really wiped us out, or maybe finally had all the energy we've been spending on the trip finally catch up with us. Whatever it was though, we decide we're going to take the day really easy and spend most of out time hanging out in the room. Luckily we have breakfast provided in the hostel, and it feels more like an empty homestay, so we don't have to spend any time socializing with other folks staying here. The breakfast is pretty good too! Pad thai, pineapple, watermelon, toast, and mango sticky rice! We're feeling brave, so after breakfast we walk into the alley across the street and walk for only a minute to get to a cute outdoor coffee shop called Sweet Home Coffee which is run by really sweet people! It was cash only and we only had a big bill to pay with, so the owner asked if we'd come back the next day and pay with a smaller bill, which was a very trusting ask! We figure we'll probably come back tomorrow anyway since it's so close so we agree. Then we go back to the room and decide to start watching The Pitt on HBO, which we quickly get into. We don't leave the room again til dinner, the brave the outside world once again for a place called It's Good Kitchen where Kaila is able to get some GF Pad See Ew and I try a regional dish called Khao Soi. It's basically a yellow curry with egg noodles in it, topped with some fried egg noodles, pickled bok choy, and shallots. Very fragrant and delicious! I get it the traditional way with beef. After dinner, it's back to the hostel for some more Pitt and then bed. A restful day!

Wednesday, May 7th - The resting continues

We sleep in again, and when we wake up, we realize we're still not quite ready to fully face the world. I'm feeling grateful that we have enough time and budget to take another day slow. There's still about a half hour left for breakfast at the hostel, so I go out and have a cup of instant coffee, more mango sticky rice, and fried rice (which is in the spot the pad thai was in yesterday). After my breakfast, Kaila and I go back to the Sweet Home Coffee place for some more coffee (and to pay for our coffee yesterday). Then we go back to the room, and finish watching The Pitt. Overall I liked the show! It was pretty graphic at times, but I liked how it was continuous and each episode was the next hour of a day in the emergency room (albeit a busy, non-standard day).

In the evening, Kaila ventures out to get her toes done at a nail salon while I get some blog time in, then I meet here and we walk over to an area that has a conglomeration of some street food vendors. On the way, we spot this cool shiny beetle on a shrub:

The street food vendors are right on the edge of the Chiang Mai Old City, so we see some of the remnants of the wall for the first time, as well as one of the only still-intact gates. I find a stall with some good-looking khao soi, this time with chicken served as a big drumstick in the noodles/curry. At first I'm wondering if I'm going to need to pick this drumstick out to eat it, but it's so tender that basically as soon as I poke it with my spoon and chopsticks, the meat falls off the bone! I think this khao soi was even better than the day before. Kaila gets some mango sticky rice.

After the food, we cross back into old town (dodging cars and motorbikes as we cross one of the many burms that now "bridges" the moat) to a Jazz bar called The North Gate Jazz Co-Op. There's no band on the main floor, but the people at the front tells us a band is playing on the fourth floor, so navigate the 3D dimensional maze of this buildings stairwells to get up their and find a nice cozy bar area buzzing with live jazz music! We get some beer and cider and watch them finish their set, then head back to the hostel.

Thursday, May 8th - Let us cook!

We wake up a bit earlier today and have some hostel breakfast. Mango sticky rice and back to pad thai today. We decide to try a new coffee shop today, so we go to a place right next door called Bloomin Moon Cafe. Today we booked a cooking class! They're picking us up at 1:30pm, so we sort of have an awkward amount of time to spend. We decide to walk more to the center of the Old City and get some Thai iced teas at a hotel cafe called Cafe De Siam. We hang out there for a bit, then walk back just in time to be picked up.

Our cooking class is a group class, so we are with seven other people. Maybe it's the rest days, but I was not able to remember or even register anyone's names. But I do remember it is a mother and daughter from Mexico, two American sisters from Minnesota, a woman from Ireland, and a couple from Germany. Our first stop as a group is to go to a market together to get the ingredients for our dishes. We have some different options, so on the drive over, our teacher (I also don't remember her name) gives us a slip of paper to choose a soup, a stir fry, a curry, and then we will all make mango sticky rice together. I choose hot and sour prawn soup, pad thai, and khao soi. Kaila chooses tom kha soup, pad thai, and panang curry.

At the market, our teacher walks us through some of the ingredients we'll all be using, then collects our slips and sends us off to explore the market for a few minutes while she collects our ingredients for us. The market has lots of fresh ingredients like spices, fruits, and vegetables, but also coffee, sweets, pastries and street food. I tried some grilled chicken wings.

After the market, our group heads to the operating space of the cooking class, which is a big, open air space that's half kitchen and half dining area. Our teacher shows around the garden in the back, which is growing herbs and some veggies. Then we all go to some chopping stations along a double sided counter and put on some aprons. First we'll be preparing the ingredients for our soups and stir fries. The teach hands out our ingredients based on which dishes we're cooking (already separated into just the right amount for us to use) and we cut them into smaller slices. Once we have our soup ingredients all chopped, we bring them over to the next station over, which is a double-sided island of burners, already equipped with a pot of water or coconut milk (based on the soup). Our teacher then guides us all to turn on the burners, and instructs us when to add certain ingredients, and brings out some chicken and shrimp, telling us when to add that as well. Now I know that mostly the steps are the same regardless of which soup we're cooking, but it's still impressive that she's keeping track of the timing for different soups when I have a hard time of even keeping track of cooking one thing.

After we finish cooking our soups, we drop them off at our place on a dining table and let the soups cool while we cook our stir fries. It's much the same steps: chopping our portioned ingredients (this time making a sauce) then following instructions of when to add the ingredients to the hot woks. When we finish these, we bring them over to join our soups, and now we can sit down to eat, enjoying the fruits of our (and our teacher's) labor, then hanging out for a while to let that food settle and give us time to get hungry for doing the same for our curry and mang sticky rice. Overall, the experience takes about four hours and ended up being a great way to spend a good chunk of the day. I'm grateful we had a good group to share it with!

We spend about an hour cooling off in our room then once the sun goes down, we brave the heat again and walk the 40 or so minutes over to the Kalare Night Market, which ends up being the most extensive night market we've seen so far. There are vendors lined up and down the sidewalks stretching a few blocks of a street, but then there are also these big structures like warehouses without walls that house dozens more vendors each, some even with rooms built in in certain places. There are probably four or five of these total, with a couple of these structures fully dedicated to street food. Multiple have live music, and one even has a boxing ring for Muay Thai fights. We wander around for a couple hours, feeling pretty full from the cooking class earlier in the day, but eventually we work up enough of an appetite for some more mango sticky rice and a beer. We sit down in an area where we can hear a rock band playing some great covers of songs from Oasis, The Black Keys, Green Day, and Linkin Park. After we hear our fill, we walk back to the hostel, feeding a family of kittens (and their mom) a meat snack along the way.

Friday, May 9th - More temples!

Today we get up early to beat the heat of the day when we hike up to a couple temples. We start with breakfast in the hostel, expecting fried rice with our mango sticky rice since yesterday we went back to pad thai, but I'm excited to see that today we have pad see ew! We grab some coffees to-go from next day then get a Grab to the trailhead (like the Minnesota folks from our cooking class suggested). We're on the trail by 8:30am, and enjoy a nice hike through the forest with soundtrack of enthusiastic cicadas. We get to the first temple, Wat Pha Lat, at 9:20am, and start exploring. It's built basically on a waterfall, though there's only a bit of water in the stream right now. We see a few monks mixed in with the tourists, and one monk speaking to a semicircle of wide-eyed tourists. We take a break and listen for a bit and hear that the monk is answering some questions from his small audience. We walk around some more, admiring the structures and the statues all around.

There's a road on the otherside of the temple that goes back down to town, and our hiking path to the temple at the top of the mountain, Wat Doi Suthep, continues just up the road a bit. We walk on the shoulder for 10ish minutes, and eventually come to it, but there is a taxi driver parked here telling us the next half of the trail is closed. This seems very convenient for the taxi driver, and the only sign we see is a piece of printer paper taped to some crossed branches in front of what appears to be the trail, so we step aside to see if we can pull up some more info on Kaila's phone. A few other hikers appear, and we clue them in to what the driver told us, then they go and talk with him for a bit. The hiking app AllTrails seems to agree about the closure, and a little bit more inspecting the area leads us to a trail further up the road that has a more legitimate trail closure sign. We negotiate with the driver some more, and agree on a price for him to take us up to the temple.

This temple is much busier! Outside, there are a bunch of shops and vendors selling snacks and souvenirs. I don't think there's much of a town up here, so all these people must be able to rely on the flow of tourists visiting the temple. There's a climb up a bunch of steps (I think I remember hearing 500 and that seems about right) from the road. A huge Naga runs along each side of the stairs, all the way to the top. The serpentine creature is plastered with green and yellow mosaic tiles. It's pretty impressive! I saw another one of those cool millipedes along it as we climbed:

Once we get to the top, we enter a whole new complex. There are many monks here, and it seems like there are living spaces for them here. There are many shrines tucked around the temple, and in the is a huge and shiny, golden stupa, the shiniest we've seen so far.

We walk around, appreciating the complexity of the place, and find a big viewing platform looking over the side of the mountain, and we can see the town in the distance. It's pretty hazy, but we can still see the airport, and a bit behind it, we can make out one of the corners of the moat demarcating the Old City.

We take the stairs back down to the road, and I find another cool arthropod on one of the Nagas again! I think this one is also a millipede, but it's a funny short and stout lil guy.

There are already some taxis waiting here, telling us the same price back to town as a Grab, but they said if we wait for more people, it will be cheaper. So we wait about 10 minutes then head down. I haven't talked about the taxis in Chiang Mai yet, but they are unique. They're pickup trucks with a roof over the bed and two benches. They're all standardized like the yellow cabs in NYC, so you see these red truck taxis all over town. Here's a view from our seats as we're heading down the mountain:

The taxis drops us off at the Baan Kang Art Village, which is another market, but this one is full of independent vendors selling more unique goods instead of the standard souvenirs and clothing we've seen at the other markets. Some folks up front have tables and tents, but most of the vendors have small brick and mortars. It really does feel like a little village, and I assume most of these little shops used to be little houses. We find a little cafe and sit down for some Thai iced teas then explore all the art! I get a couple fun enamel pins and a cool corduroy five panel with three gooses embroidered on the front and the text "ALL GOOSE :)". The artist we got the hat from had some really cool stuff! It was fun wandering through the vendors spots for a couple hours, and the general vibe of this place was just very pleasant.

Now it's time for food! There's a Mediteranean spot on the other side of town called Sababa where Kaila saw randomly has really good GF pita, so we get a grab over there so Kaila can get a shawarma sandwich (that she said was really good). I get some fries, but I see this as a good opportunity to try the fancy chicken & rice bowl they have at the KFCs in Thailand (we saw an IG video about this). So after the Mediteranean food, we walk 10 minutes over to a KFC in a mall and I try the chicken zabb bowl, which is surprisingly good. It's rice with small pieces of fried chicken and onions on top and a dusting of a blend of dried chilis and spices. And it only cost 89 baht, which is like 2.71 USD.

Since we're already in the mall, we check out a grocery store that's also inside, get some snacks for later, and get a Grab back to the hostel. We hang out for a couple hours cooling down and I get some blog time in. When it's dark again, we go back out to walk to dinner. Kaila gets some pad thai at a place on the edge of Old City that has good gluten free reviews called Grazie Thai Kitchen and the server is really nice and brings out the fish sauce and soy sauce to show Kaila that they're both GF. The restaurant appears to be full of British people, and Kaila jokes that the Chiang Mai Mad Monkey must be close, but then we check and it is actually right across the Old City moat from here which is pretty hilarious that she called that. Since we're back close to the Kalare Night Market, I decide I want to try some of the street food I was too full to try yesterday, so we walk back over there and I get some honey grilled pork and some fermented pork sausage.

Yesterday when we were at the market, there were a bunch of people out promoting the Muay Thai fights that are happening at the boxing stadium here tonight. This isn't the kind of thing Kaila and I typically are into, but it feels like one of those "when in Rome" type moments where we realize this is an opportunity to do something we'll probably never do again. The fights start in just an hour, so we decide to hang around until then. We spot the Americans and the Irish woman from the cooking class so we go over and chat with them for a bit, hearing about their day since the class before we head to the fight.

We weren't really sure what to expect from the fights, although we definitely weren't expecting the fight to start with two 10 year olds, which is exactly how it started. Neither of us knew how Muay Thai works at all, so we were glad to see boxing gloves. After the kids went, we borrowed an info sheet one of the groups sitting next to us had about the fights & we saw that the rest of the fights would be people in higher weight classes & presumably adults. It was still pretty brutal when the hard punches or kicks landed, but all the fighters seemed very respectful to each other. It was also surprising to see the personalized rituals the fighters do before each match. It was also interesting that there were drums playing in the background during the fights, and the fighters seemed to bounce along to them. We thought we would only stay for three or four fights, but we actually stayed for almost all of them (there were six or seven) and we didn't get back to our room til midnight, which was maybe not the best idea because we still had to pack our bags for our bus ride to Chiang Rai in the morning...

Saturday, May 10th -

Today we woke up late! Maybe not a surprise since we stayed up late last night, but we were planning to wake up at 7am and instead we woke up at 7:44 and jumped out of bed. Luckily we packed most of our stuff up last night, so only had to do a bit of frantic packing. Our bus is leaving at 9am, but we have to get over to the bus station first. Somehow, we get all our things together by 8am and still have time to eat some quick breakfast (pad see ew again with the mango sticky rice) before we get a Grab over to the bus station.

It's really not so much of a bus station as it is an Indian restaurant. It's actually not a bus station at all. If we had not booked our tickets through 12co, the online bus booking platform for Asia, I would have been worried, but there were plenty of good reviews for the company we chose. We get over there at 8:35am, which is just enough time to use the bathroom and get some coffee before we get into the mini-bus and depart at 9:05am. We make a couple stops on our four hour journey to Chiang Rai: one is planned and where I have my first squatting toilet experience, the next is spur of the moment because someone on the bus isn't feeling well. This leads Kaila and me into a rabbit hole of figuring out how often it has been for us that someone has gotten sick on the bus. There have been four occurences and 15 bus rides, so that's about 26.67%. Not great numbers!

We arrive to Baan Mai Kradan Hostel around 1pm, then hang out in the lobby for an hour while we wait for our room to be ready. This place is cool! The common area actually feels like somewhere I'd like to hang out, and there's a nice yard space out front with plenty of shade and tables and chairs. There's even a friendly hostel cat who we meet! We eventually get up to our room, and it's cute too! Big windows and a nice bathroom. Good all around!

We hang out in the room for a bit, but it's still really hot because the AC was turned off, so we decide to walk over to 7-Eleven to get some snacks while the room is cooling down. We eat them in the nice common area downstairs, then go back up to the room to hang out for a bit (good for blog time). There's a night market that happens every Saturday and it's only a few minutes walk from our hostel, so we go check it out. It's huge!

It's really different than the Kalare market we went to in Chiang Mai. There is a bunch of food, but hardly any of the signs are in English, which makes sense because most of the people walking around seem to be Thai. There are still clothing vendors, but it's more day-to-day clothes like t-shirts and jeans. There's also a hug stage in the middle with some folks signing karaoke with people dancing in front and a bunch of tables and chairs for others to watch while eating their street food. We walk around and scope out a bunch of the street food, and eventually I choose a few things to eat: a flat grilled pork skewer, a bowl of breaded pork with riceberries and chilis, and a caramelized chicken liver skewer. It's all so tasty! The pork skewer is a nice combination of sweet and savory. The riceberries in the bowl of pork pretty much just taste like rice and is very spicy; I have to take a break after eating it (worth it). The chicken liver is my favorite, though. I love the taste of liver on it's own, but these grilled skewers have a sweet and crispy outer layer that has caramelized on the outside. I will be thinking about these skewers for a long time.

The street food is a big unknown as far as safety for Kaila goes, so we walk through to a Thai restaurant called Awe's Cafe a few blocks away that Kaila saw a review for on her GF app. The owner is really nice & says he had a boyfriend that was gluten free so knows exactly what she can't eat. She orders some panang curry and it's good and doesn't make her sick! After Kaila gets her food, we go back to the market, walk the rest of the market then get some mango sticky rice and sit down to watch the people sing and dance for a bit longer before heading back to the hostel and turning in for the night.

Sunday, May 11th -

We sleep in a bit today because we don't have anything planned except going to see a temple in the afternoon. I go down stairs to get an omelet and some toast for breakfast (included in our stay), then we walk over to a place called the Kalae Cafe to get some coffee and some fruit and yogurt for Kaila. It's starting to look like rain, so we walk back to the hostel. It's starting to get windy too so it really feels like something is rolling in. Almost as soon as we get back to the hostel, it starts pouring. There's a common area outside our room on the second floor that's open-air and looks out over the front yard, so we hang out here a bit and read/blog.

Once the rain stops, we go to 7-Eleven for some snacks. It's supposed to rain some more, so we decide to hang around the hostel more to watch the weather a bit before going out again. After an hour, there's still no rain, so we decide to take a Grab out to Wat Rong Suea Ten Temple, also known as the Blue Temple. This is a new temple, only completed in 2016, so it's interesting to see a more modern take on the Buddhist art. The Wikipedia article describes it as "unconventional 'neo-traditional' style of Buddhist art . . . employing lavishly ornamented sculptures and psychedelic visual imagery". I think psychedelic is a surprisingly accurate description. Some of the art inside the temple reminds me of work by artists like Alex Grey.

We were pretty quick seeing the Blue Temple, so we decide we'll also go check out Wat Huay Pla Kang. This temple has a 26 story statue of the Goddess of Mercy as well as a 9 story pagoda, both of which you can go to the top of. The Goddess statue we don't actually climb, but the elevator up is only 40 baht (like 1.20 USD). Once we get up to the head, the elevator opens to a room full of white statues that blend into the walls. Dragons, trees with detailed foliage. We can see out a circular window on the forehead which we saw from the ground, but the eyes are also windows! Pretty cool. Around the back of the head, we got a cool view of the mountains too.

After the Goddess of Mercy, we head over to the pagoda. There are some huge dragons on either side of the stairs going to the base, and I'm surprised at some other statues we find in front of these stairs...

I have a hard time finding info about this temple online, so I really have no idea why these statues are here. We continue into the pagoda and can actually climb the stairs here. At the top, there are windows all the way around, so we decide to stay here to watch the sunset. The blue layer effect of the mountains get more intense, and we watch a bunch of kids running around the grounds of the temple. We can't tell if the area is also a school, or maybe if the kids are on some kind of a school trip or camp or something, but it seems like something is going on. Anyway, the sunset is nice!

We get a grab back to our hostel to change out of our long pants, then we walk to get some dinner. The place we went to last night is closed today and Kaila has read some horror stories of gluten cross contamination in Thailand, so decide to go to one of the few restaurants on her GF app that are open. It's an Italian restaurant called La Gatta. We haven't had any pasta in Asia yet, but some cheese sounds good (it's one of the things I miss the most from back home). I get a gnocchi dish with a chicken parm thing and some vodka sauce and Kaila gets some GF pasta with red sauce and mushrooms. I forget that today is a Buddhist holiday, so I order a beer and they ask if they can bring it in a mug. Hopefully it wasn't rude to still order it! Once we finish eating, we walk back to the hostel and get ready for bed.

Monday, May 12th - Staving off illness

Today Kaila wakes up feeling like she's coming down with a bit of a cold. The plans for our next leg of the trip is to take a van to the border with Laos then get a slow boat that travels down the Mekong River, stops in the Laos town Pak Seng, for the night, then continues to the city Luang Prabang. Kaila wants to feel 100% for that trip, so we decide to take a rest day so she can get ahead of it. We book another night at the hostel so we can do the last temple we want to see tomorrow and leave for Laos the following day. We eat our omelets provided in our stay then Kaila goes back up to the room and I walk to get us some coffee from a place called Alio. The coffee is really good! We drink it in the room and start watching the new season of Top Chef, which is in Canada. I catch up on the blog on and off while we watch.

A dinner place that had been closed all week is open today, but Kaila isn't feeling good enough to go out, so we try the food option on Grab and someone on a motorbike brings us some GF pad thai and "northen Thailand noodle curry" which is basically khao soi and is pretty good (although it was better in Chiang Mai). Though I guess we never had the curry in Chiang Mai brought to us! We eat in the hostel yard then watch one more episode of Top Chef before going to bed.

Tuesday, May 13th -

This morning Kaila still isn't feeling good so we book another night at the hostel so we can push our temple plans and Laos departure back a day (perhaps we'll be here forever?). I eat my omelet, sharing one of the accompanying chicken sausages with a guy named Jan who is from Germany, and plans to leave for Laos in a day or so. Then I go get coffee, yogurt, and fruit for Kaila to eat in the room. The plan today is more Top Chef and blogging!

In the evening, Kaila gives me a hair cut in the room, then I go off on a solo mission to get some food at the Night Bazaar. The Night Bazaar is open every night of the week, and has some small brick and mortar stalls lining the edge of a few connected corners in the center of the city. There is a big outdoor restaurant, and an outdoor food court/hall area that has dozens of food-dedicated stalls around a big area of tables and chairs with a big stage on one end. We had checked this out on Saturday, but that was during the Saturday market, so it had seemed a little lackluster in comparison. But now that it's the only game in town (only night market open right now), it has the attention of the city. Even in the early evening, it's pretty busy! I do a couple laps, while some beach rock renditions of western songs play over the speakers from the stage. A really funny slow and acoustic version of "Bring me to Life" originally by Evanesence comes on and cracks me up. Eventually I decide on a barbecue place that let's you pick your items and they grill it for you on the spot. I got mushrooms, chicken, fish balls, crocodile, and some of the caramelized flat pork. They give it to me with some sticky rice and peanut sauce, and I sit down to eat while a guy gets on the stage to play some classical guitar.

The restaurant we ordered food from last night is close, so I pick up some more GF pad thai for Kaila and some mango sticky rice from a street vendor and head back to the hostel. Kaila meets me downstairs and she eats the pad thai and we share the mango sticky rice at one of the tables in the hostel's yard. It rained a bit earlier but has since cleared up, so the air is a bit cooler than it has been. The cicadas are chirping, and we can hear the calls of some frogs or toads that must be close. Really close! Eventually we see one hopping not too far away and I'm able to get a pretty good photo. They're pretty big, nearly the size of a tennis ball!

While I was gone, Kaila started Tina Fey's new show which is a miniseries about marriage and relationships of some friends in their mid-lives. She says she thinks I would like it, so once we finish eating, we continue watching. Tina Fey stars in it with Steve Carell and Will Forte. We end up finishing it, and I did like it! Very funny, but also serious at the same time, and I would say especially relevant for married couples. I would recommend it. After we finish the show, we call it a night.

Wednesday, May 14th -

Today Kaila is feeling a lot better! We go downstairs to eat our breakfast (omelet for me and Kaila finishes her leftover pad thai from last night) then we officially book the slow boat into Laos for the next day! We are going to leave, after all. We go get some coffee at the Kalae Cafe, then walk over to the bus station where we catch a retro bus over to the temple we're going to see today.

The temple we're going to is called Wat Rong Khun, also known as the White Temple. This temple is also a modern temple, built in 1997. It is very white, and many of the details on the outside of the temple are lined with mirrors that reflect the sunlight in a way that makes the temple feel even more impossibly white.

To enter the temple, we follow a path which crosses an area that resembles to be a pit with hundreds of sculpted hands reaching out. Around the border are sculpted skulls and more hands, the stone connecting them appear web-like, as if it is all bound together by melted flesh or decaying organic material. It's clear that this area is meant to be a depiction of Hell.

Once we're on the other side of the path crossing the pit, there's a bridge that spans a koi pond and deposits us right in the front of the temple. The temple seems even more beautiful after the walk through the hellish garden, which I'm sure is the point. We take off our shoess and walk into the temple to see the shrine to Buddha in front of us with the walls and ceiling depicting Buddhist imagery in a modern way that isn't as psychedelic as the Blue Temple. At least not immediately so.

Photos were not allowed inside the temple, which is unfortunate because when we turned around to see the wall opposite the shrine, the first thing we notice is flames, horns, and monsters, and we think the wall is another representation of Hell. But it stranger than that, because let your eyes linger a bit longer, and you start to recognize more in this wall. On the left side of the door, one of the large horns coming up from the flames at the bottom has a fancy watch around it, and a red laser tracks it from giant missile. There's a giant hand with pointer finger that becomes an assault rifle. A volcano erupts in front of a Mayan pyramid, beside which a tentacle reaches out into space shown above and wraps around a US space shuttle in front of the Earth peppered with giant explosions. The scene gets even stranger the more you look: Kung Fu Panda can be seen striking a pose, along with Keanu Reeves as Neo, Bumblebee from Tranformers, Sailor Moon. There are some swirls of detail that look more traditionally Buddhist, but inside them poke out fighter jets, and a classical hand clutches a Nokia phone. Michael Jackson even makes it on the wall.

That's just one side of the wall. The right side is even stranger, because the focal point here is an 4+ foot tall rendition of the Twin Towers mid-collapse, with a plane about to hit the second tower, and, get this, AN ANGRY BIRD RIGHT BEHIND THE SECOND PLANE! Truly unhinged. There's also a snake turning into a gas pump over here with a pikachu dancing on it, Jack Sparrow and a pirate ship being attacked by a kraken, and also Godzilla, but these all seem less jarring compared to the 9/11 portrayal. Part of this side of the mural actually has a photo up on the Wikipedia page:

After leaving the inside of the temple, we walked around it some more to admire it from a few more angles, then we left the temple complex and headed back towards the highway where the bus dropped us off, following some loose directions given to us by someone at the temple ticket stand. This ended up being less of a station and more of a few chairs under an awning beside a police station, but there was a woman waiting there too that told us we were in the right place, or at least seemed to think we were.

Thursday, May 15th - To the river!

Today we wake up at 4:15am so that we have enough time to finish packing before a minivan picks us up at the hostel and brings us to the Thailand-Laos border where we will take a "slow boat" down the river to the Laos city Luang Prabang. We go down to the lobby at 5 and see Jan, the guy I shared a chicken sausage with a couple breakfasts ago, and Cas from the Netherlands, who will be joining us on the minivan. We are the first to be picked up, so we get the first pick of seats (except for Cas who the driver makes sit in the passenger seat because he is seven feet tall). We pick up six more people. It's very early so the ride starts off quiet, but eventually I learn they are Sam and Thom from the Nethlands, Lisa and Yoan from France, and two solo travelers whose names I never got.

The ride to the border takes about two hours, and we get to know each other a bit more as we all follow each other through the steps to legally exit Thailand and enter Laos. We will board the boat to Luang Prabang soon!
