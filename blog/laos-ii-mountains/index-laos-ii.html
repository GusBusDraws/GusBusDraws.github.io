Laos II: Mountains!

Published Monday, April 7, 2025

<<< >>>

Mon May 19 - Arriving in Nong Khiaow

Today we're taking a bus to Nong Khiaow, a small town surrounded by mountains in northern Laos. Kaila and I take a bus from Luang Prabang with our new friends Canadian Sam and Australian Josh, who we met on the slow boat into Laos from north Thailand. We decided the four of us will do the Thakhek motorbike loop in central Laos in a couple weeks, so we've decided to travel together until then. Almost as soon as we get on the bus, we meet another travel who begins talking about the loop. His name is Andy, and he lives in Vancouver, like Sam. At first we're open to him joining us, but he then starts quizzing us about the exact day we will start the loop. The four of us don't have a set schedule yet as we all have some flexibility in this part of our trip, but Andy doesn't really take this as an answer. He keeps pushing for May 24th, eventually moving it back to May 26th, and we keep trying to politely tell him it will around then but we'll work out a particular day as we get closer. Eventually he gets pushy to the point where I tell him he doesn't have the negotiation power here since he is the single traveler that is trying to join the four of us. He sort of drops it, but still jokingly brings it up a few times. A bit later in the ride, the minivan stops at a village and I think maybe some cargo is loaded/unloaded, but Andy decides its a good time to get out and buy some donuts, which felt pretty bold at the time since we weren't sure how long we were stopping for, but then he shares the donuts with us when he gets back on which is nice. At this point we are already feeling like Andy is a character, but then he starts talking some more about the loop and somehow the topic of drinking comes up and he says something about drinking while on motorbikes and none of us can actually tell if he's joking. I laugh but he only doubles down saying they don't have checkpoints here and it's easy to drive a motorbike while drunk and it just doesn't seem like a funny joke if it even is one, but eventually the topic changes again. Andy isn't sitting directly next to us, so the conversation doesn't naturally continue, which is good because it's four and a half hours all said and done before we get off the van in Nong Khiaow.

Most everyone at this point gets a tuk tuk to their hostels from here, but we are hungry and decide to grab some lunch close by then walk the half hour across the town to our hostel on the otherside. Andy is staying somewhere else, so he departs, but he exchanges socials with Sam beforehand while making sure to say "Thakhek on the 26th!" as he lives, which we are all understandably even more skeptical about now. Another traveler from the van, Jenny from Germany, asks us where we're staying and how we're planning on getting there. She's staying at the hostel next door and decides to join us for lunch then walk with us. We find a place close by called Mr. Wan's where most of us get a rice or curry dish and eat our food. After we finished, we standing around and a guy that was working at the restaurant starts chatting with us. He tells us about his favorite peak hike in the area, which looks like it' starts from right over by our hostel. We make note of that for later and start our walk across town.

The town is very charming. It feels small, with most of the buildings only being a floor or two. It seems like it's mostly local-serving, with some restaurants and cafes and shops, but not really any hotels. There are some guesthouses sprinkled throughout, but not many. It sort of matches what I expect since it seems like many folks who visit here do a tour from Luang Prabang instead of coming into the town on their own. Many of the hostels seem to be across the river on the otherside of town, so I assume a lot of the tourist infrastructure is probably concentrated over there, too. But it's nice, and the nature is gorgeous already. All around us, the round limestone mountains seem to pop up from the valley, and they all seem massive around us.

On our walk, we stop for some ice cream from a shop and have a nice lil photo op to show off the ice cream in front of the mountains. I finally try some of the corn flavored ice cream, and it's actually really tasty! I guess corn is naturally pretty sweet, so making it even sweeter for ice cream isn't as crazy as it first seemed to me. It even has this funny little inverted cone/waffle thing around it which makes it seem even more like an ear of corn.

We get to our hostel, which is called Eco Farmstay and Hostel, and it's pretty much exactly what you would expect. There's a big open air reception/lounge/restaurant that has a walkway from an elevated road so that it's actually up on the second floor looking out at a field it's set on. An Australian guy checks us in and shows us the dorms directly below reception where Sam and Josh are staying, the central, shared showers and bathrooms, and the bamboo-walled single rooms off behind the bathrooms where Kaila and I are staying. We all drop off our stuff, then look at the map and confirm to confirm that the peak hike, Som Nang, is close by (it is) and book it over there so we can make it up before the sun sets, grabbing few Beerlao cans on our way to have some summit beers. As we're hiking up, Sam realizes he has some messages from Andy on IG about him bringing over some beer to our hostel. Same tells him about the hike and that he could join if he wants, but we are already in a hurry to get to the top. And it's good we were rushing, because we make it to the top right as we start getting color in the sky from the sunset. The view is magnificient! We are facing west looking over the town, with it looking tiny around the tall mountains. We definitely aren't on the tallest peak, but it was sort of the perfect hike to get a great view in the limited time we had.

We make our way down from the peak which is a bit treachorous since it's now getting dark. The trailhead is right at the bridge back to the main part of town, so we decide to cross over and get some dinner at an open air restaurant called 2 Sisters Restaurant. It's around this time that Sam realizes he's missed some more messages from Andy, about how he's bringing up a full case of beer to the viewpoint, then how he got there and couldn't find us. They have an akward back and forth where it's still not clear if Andy is joking, or if there was a miscommunication and he went to the wrong viewpoint hike. We are chatting about this and heading back and Sam is feeling a mix of confused and guilty because he doesn't know if he made a mistake, but we look back and his messages seem clear to us. So we're walking back to our hostel and reflecting on the strange interactions we've had with Andy then Sam realizes he has another message from Andy about how he's drinking alone at a bar in town when suddenly we see Andy appear up ahead of us in front of a bar. We talk to him for a bit and half-apologize, half-question if he actually did the things he said, but then tell him we're gonna go back to our hostel and go to bed since we're all pretty exhausted. The strangeness continues!

We get back to the hostel and we all head to the showers, which is funny because none of the walls connect to the ceilings and we can all still chat with each other while we get ready for bed. The open air showers also means that there are bugs everywhere, including big moths, catydids, and some really annoying mayfly type things. Once we get past this, we all go to bed.

Monday, May 26 - Off to the capital

Today we're heading down to the capital as a stepping stone to do some more nature stuff farther south. Josh booked us all (Kaila, Sam, Josh, and Jarryd) train tickets for 1:30pm, so we have the morning to get things in order and get some food before we head out. Kaila and I mostly pack our bags first thing, with the plan that we can just come grab them quickly after breakfast. We decide to do breakfast at Pullmind Cafe today since we finished out AirBnB eggs and bread yesterday. Sam goes early to talk to a friend on the phone, so Josh, Jarryd, and I take the three motorbikes we rented back to the shop and get the two passports back that they kept for collateral. Kaila goes to the cafe while we do this, then Jarryd and I meet her there. FOr breakfast, I get "egg bread" which is a plate of toast, tomatoes, bacon, avocado, and lettuces covered in mayo (basically a build your own BLt with scrambled eggs an the side). I also order a Malibu Latte thinking it will be something tropical, but the first sip I take is almost exclusively Malibu rum, which everyone else thinks I am dumb for not expecting. Jarryd has his own coffee surprise when he orders a coconut latte with an extra shot of espresso and appears to get two lattes instead. Kaila asks for steamed rice with a fried egg and is told she can't have that, then orders the egg bread I got but without the bread, and ends up getting the egg bread with rice instead of bread. A morning of misunderstandings!

By now it's almost noon which is when we need to be out of the AirBnB, so I leave breakfast early to get back and finish packing. Then as a group, we have a whirlwind time of finding loose things around the main floor and finally get out around 12:30pm. Close enough! There is a truck tuk outside that we hire to take us to the train station. When we get on the train, Josh pulls out the leftover Somersbys for us. After we've been drinking them for a little while, one of the train attendants comes over and tells us alcohol is not allowed on the train and to not let the police see, which is a funny alternative to telling us to stop drinking them. Sam does a crossword on the train, and at some point asks what shape Utah is, which I for some reason thought was very funny. It's only about an hour and a half to Vientiane, so we'll be there soon!
