Borneo II: South Sabah

This post covers the adventures of my wife, Kaila, and I through the south of the Malaysian state Sabah, which is located on the island Borneo. This includes a snorkeling day trip to three islands off the coast of the town Semporna and a three day, 2 night trip in the primary rainforest of Tawau Hills Park, just outside the town of Tawau.

Thu Jun 12 - Arriving in Semporna, Sabah

Fri Jun 13 - Snorkeling

Today we're going snorkeling on a multi-island trip we booked through BorneoTravelNetwork.com. We get up early to pack a daypack and go to a restaurant in front of the hotel where I get some chicken fried rice and Kaila gets an omelet. A van picks us up from our hotel, then drives us over to the boat jetty where we get on a boat operated by a company called Dive Monsters. The first stop is on an island called Timba Timba, which has a small forested area with a couple small houses and a long sandbar on either side. The tour guides tell us the stop is to take photos on the beach, which is kind of annoying but much of the group seems pretty into it. After some time here, we get back on the boat and go to the otherside of the island for snorkeling. The reef is really interesting! The coral is alive, which is not something we've seen before snorkeling in the Guld of Mexico. We see a lot of tropical fish, including some big angel fish.

The next stop is Mataking island, a bigger island with a larger forested area that has an area with many picnic tables where the companies bring their groups to eat lunch. Our group gets lunches from metal trays packed by the Green Cafe, which is funny because this restaurant was right next door to the restaurant we ate breakfast at, but wasn't open yet, probably because they were busy making these lunches! The meal consists of rice, veggies, a slice of omelet, and some fried chicken. We have some free time on island after lunch, which we spend walking on the side of the island opposite the boats, which has a beautiful beach with coarse sand. There are lots of little crabs here, including some tiny hermit crabs!

After lunch, we go to a snorkel spot on the other side of the island and see even more living coral! The coral here is even more colorful and varied, with tons of different shapes: trees-like, round, big plates (which when you look closely can also see has a similar branching form that has grown into a flat plane), and even some brain coral. We also see some anenomes, one that has a couple clown fish living in it! There's even blue tangs here, which add to the Finding Nemo vibes.

The last island we go to is Pandanan Island. The snorkel spot here is right on the edge of a steep drop-off into much deeper ocean, which means there are some really interesting views of the variety of coral and tropical life with a deep blue background. It feels a bit silly, but it made me feel like I was in a screensaver from the early 2000's. Kaila had a much more elegant description of feeling like we are in a nature documentary. There is some truth to this actually, because much of David Attenborough's documentary Blue Planet 2 was filmed off the coast of Sidapan Island, just a short boat ride from here! Another interesting part of this reef is feeling many different water currents. As we swam around, we would enter some areas where the water felt hot or very cold, and it would change quickly without much of a gradient. Kaila said she thought she could see the currents, and looking that up later, apparently that can be possible.

After this last bit of snorkeling, we get back in the boat, wait for some scuba divers to rejoin who split off earlier, then we head back to Semporna. Once we're there, Kaila and I decide to walk over to the place on the map marked as the Tawau Bus Station right by a KFC in town to see if we can buy bus tickets to Tawau for tomorrow. Unfortunately, there isn't anybody there. I remember the tour guide telling us that there may be buses to Tawau by one of the Shell gas stations, and see that there's something else marked as the Tawau Van Terminal nearby, so we walk over there. We find a man on the curb by some vans and he tells us there aren't tickets for the vans, people just show up and the van leaves when it's full. He says we should show up an hour or so before we want to leave. Now that we know this, we start walking the 15 minutes back to our hotel, stopping at TwenTea again on the way. This time I got a cheesy matcha with boba that had a savory cheese foam on the top that had a taste almost like cottage cheese, but a bit sweeter and with a totally smooth texture (i.e. no chunks). After we get back to the room and shower, Kaila realizes she got a reply back from the 1StopBorneo folks, and they have an opening for us to do a three day, two night tour at their accomodations at the Tawau Hills Park. We weren't planning on doing another tour, but a few days in the primary rainforest of Tawau Hills Park is too good to pass up, and we think we can fit it into the rest of our budget for Malaysia. We book the tour, and head back to Summer Breeze Cafe so Kaila can get some more of the lamb that didn't make her sick. I get a fried chicken sandwich, and it's so good! Crispy on the outside, but not dry on the inside, with lots of flavor and a spicy sauce. One of the best chicken sandwiches I think I've had, after a couple restaurants in Washington, DC. After dinner, we walk back to the room, watch the season finale of Top Chef that just came out, and go to bed.

Sat Jun 14 - Minivan to Tawau, Sabah

We wake at 6am to give us enough time to finish packing and get over to the area the vans for Tawau leave from around 7:30. We grab some more breakfast at the place we ate yesterday morning, but this time I get some stir fried noodles with chicken. We walk over to the vans, getting around 7:45, and we are the first people there. The guy tells us we still need to wait for eight more people, which will probably take about an hour. This doesn't seem like a problem, since the 1StopBorneo folks said they could have a car pick us up from Tawau at 1pm. We decide to walk over to a 7-Eleven and grab some bottled coffee to bring back. When we get back, there are two more people on the van. Progress!

We have ten people by 9am, and head off for Tawau. The van ride is actually very comfortable, with strong AC and plenty of leg room. We arrive at 11:30, which gives us plenty of time to get some food and bug spray from a grocery store before we head into the jungle. We can't find a whole lot that Kaila can eat, but there is more cheddar cheese here and some tortilla chips, so we get that and some more Spam-like canned meat like we eat the other day and decide to find a place to have a little Lunchables-style picnic. We're close to the shore, so we walk over there and find a concrete retaining wall/bench along a foot path by a rocky shore. We sit down, and immediately realize there are rats running around the rocks. Luckily the retaining wall is much higher on the rocky side so we don't feel threated by the rats, but they definitely smell our food and I have to jump down and scare some of the braver individuals a few times. Otherwise the view is nice! We also see some lizards jumping around, and on the rocks at the water's edge, we can even see some mudskippers hopping around!

Eventually it gets close enough to 1pm that we have to leave and walk over to the pick-up spot where we'll meet our driver, Mr. Wong. When he picks us up, he's a sweet older, Malaysian man. He chats with us a bit at the beginning of the drive, and quickly we're asking him all kinds of questions about Sabah. We learn a lot from him! He tells us a bit about the relationship between the palm oil industry and the timber industry, and how most of the palm oil plantations came in after the forests were already cut down for timber. He tells us about British colonization, and agreements between the UK and Japan led logging and agriculture by both empires, with Tawau Hills park being protected as a way to protect the water supply from the mountains, since freshwater was otherwise harder to find in the flat land closer to the sea around Tawau. There was even a Japanese freight rail that used to run in the same place as the road we are driving from Tawau to the Park. It's a lot to think about, and it makes me excited to talk to the tour guides to learn even more.

We get to the park a bit before 2, and in the parking lot we're immediately greeted by two guys from 1StopBorneo: Shavez and Chun (rhymes with tune). They are excitedly telling us to get out of the car to see something. It's a red langur! A very fuzzy monkey whose diet consists mostly of leaves. Shavez is on his way out but says he'll be back tonight, but Chun sticks around and tells us he'll take us to the accomodations so we can drop off our bags. It's a ten minute walk over, and on the way, we see a big water monitor, probably 1.5 meters long, on the paths. Already two cool wildlife sightings!

The accomodations are nice! They are made up of a big central shared space with three long tables, one of which has a couple laptops, notebooks, cameras, binoculars, and is otherwise covered by hard drives. Chun says there are at least 40 terrabytes of data here, containing mostly photos and videos that have either been taken by 1StopBorneo staff and visiting scientists, or that have been captured by various camera traps that have been placed around the park. On one side of the room are some plates and cups and a refrigerator. In one corner is a TV, and opposite that is a table of books including guide books for various animals of Borneo, and the first field guide ever written for Tawau Hills Park, written only a few years ago by the 1StopBorneo staff. There are also some magazines that the group has started publishing annually (the first three volumes are available for free online), and a big binder containing newspaper articles tracing back to the beginning of the group. There are six or eight rooms on each side of the common area.

Some more info on 1StopBorneo: They are a staff of about 10 people that operate a non-governmental organization and tour company under the same name. Beyond tourism, they do conservation work at a few different sites across Borneo, with one of the sites being Tawau Hills Park. They are separate from the Park itself, although they partner with them closely, including running the park's accomodation and having access to the park outside of normal hours. They also partner with other organizations, mostly locally, but also with the Malaysian chapter of the World Wildlife Foundation (WWF). Besides tourists, they also host students and scientists at their operation sites and help them perform science and conservation work. Some very cool things happening here!

After Chun shows us to our room, he tells us we have a couple hours before our first activity: an evening walk through the rainforest to see the animals as they start to become more active. We take this time to rest a bit and read some of the 1StopBorneo materials in their little library.

Once it's time for our afternoon walk with Chun, he lends us a pair of binculars to share and we head off. Pretty close to camp, we see some pretty big ants that have a bit of yellow to them. Chun says these are golden fish hook ants, and tells us to take a closer look and try to see the two sets of spikes they have on their backs to deter predators. Kaila got a really good picture of them!
